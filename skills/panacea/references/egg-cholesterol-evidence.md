> Key question: should users with elevated LDL-C limit whole eggs?

## Core Mechanism

Dietary cholesterol ≠ blood cholesterol. Blood LDL-C is primarily driven by
**saturated fat** and **trans fat** intake, not dietary cholesterol. One large
egg contains ~200 mg dietary cholesterol but only ~1.5 g saturated fat — far
less than equivalent-calorie portions of red meat, butter, or processed meats.

The liver tightly regulates endogenous cholesterol synthesis; in most people,
increased dietary cholesterol is compensated by reduced hepatic production
(AHA 2019 Science Advisory, *Circulation*).

## Key Evidence

### Carter 2025 RCT (AJCN) — the study users see as "eggs lowered my LDL"

- Design: randomized cross-over, n=61, 5 wk per diet: EGG (600 mg/d cholesterol,
  6% energy SFA, 2 eggs/d) vs EGG-FREE (300 mg/d, 12% SFA, no eggs) vs CON
  (600 mg/d, 12% SFA, 1 egg/wk)
- Result: LDL-C CON 109.3 → EGG 103.6 mg/dL (−5.7, p=0.02); EGG-FREE 107.7 (NS)
- Within-study: LDL correlated with **saturated fat** (β=0.35, p=0.002), NOT with
  dietary cholesterol (β=−0.006, p=0.42)
- Caveats: small, non-blinded, Egg Nutrition Center funded, erratum Dec 2025;
  EGG increased small LDL particles (β=+95.1, p=0.004) and reduced large ones
- PMID 40339906. Lesson: the LDL drop came from the low-SFA background diet.

### Other "no harm" trials do not show eggs lowering LDL

- PROSPERITY (Am Heart J 2025;279:27-39, PMID 39414223): ≥12 fortified eggs/wk
  vs <2/wk, 4 mo → LDL −3.14 mg/dL (NS); Eggland's Best funded.
- Yale-Griffin DASH trial (J Am Nutr Assoc 2026;45(3):237-249, PMID 40957619):
  DASH + 2 eggs/d vs DASH alone in 45 hyperlipidemic adults → LDL numerically
  +4.2 mg/dL in the egg arm (p=0.317).

### Dose-response (meta-analyses of RCTs)

- Vincent 2019 meta-regression (55 trials, n=2652, PMID 30596814): +100 mg/d
  dietary cholesterol → LDL +1.90 (linear) / +4.46–4.58 mg/dL (non-linear);
  saturating across 0–1500 mg/d.
- Rouhani 2018 (28 RCTs, PMID 29111915): eggs vs control → LDL +5.55, HDL +2.13
  mg/dL; LDL/HDL and TG unchanged.
- Khalighi Sikaroudi 2020 (66 RCTs, PMID 32524644): >1 egg/d may raise TC/LDL-C/
  HDL-C; ratio unchanged; non-linear LDL dose-response.
- Berger 2015 (PMID 26109578): dietary cholesterol ↑LDL 6.7 mg/dL overall, but NS
  when intervention doses exceeded 900 mg/d.

### Chinese guideline stance (locally applicable — differs from US DGA)

- 《中国血脂管理指南（基层版 2024）》(中国循环杂志 2024;39(4)): ASCVD 中危以上
  或合并高胆固醇血症者应控制膳食胆固醇 **<300 mg/天**（≈1 egg）; 油脂 20–25 g/d;
  replace SFA with unsaturated fat. US 2020–2025 DGA dropped the numeric cap.
  Present both; do not silently adopt the US position for a Chinese user.

### ACC PROSPERITY Trial (2024)

- Design: RCT, 140 patients with CVD or ≥2 risk factors, 4 months
- Intervention: ≥12 fortified eggs/week vs <2 eggs/week
- Results: LDL-C -3.14 mg/dL (NS), HDL-C -0.64 mg/dL (NS)
- Secondary: numerical reductions in total cholesterol, apoB, LDL particle
  number, hs-troponin, and insulin resistance
- Conclusion: **No adverse effect on blood lipids** even in high-risk population
- Limitation: small (n=140), unblinded, funded by Eggland's Best
- Source: Nouhravesh et al., ACC.24 (2024-03-28)

### AHA 2019 Science Advisory

- Systematic review of observational and intervention studies
- "Studies have not generally supported an association between dietary
  cholesterol and cardiovascular risk"
- Healthy adults: up to **1 whole egg/day**
- Older adults with healthy cholesterol: up to **2 eggs/day**
- Source: Carson et al., *Circulation* 2019;140(00):e000–e000

### AHA 2023 Update (Van Horn)

- Emphasized that eggs are high in dietary cholesterol but **low in saturated
  fat** — the real driver of LDL-C
- "The problem is what people eat alongside their eggs — buttered toast, bacon,
  processed meats"
- Re-affirmed 1 egg/day for healthy adults
- Source: AHA News, 2023-08-25

### 2020-2025 US Dietary Guidelines

- Removed the 300 mg/day dietary cholesterol limit
- Recommendation: keep dietary cholesterol "as low as possible without
  compromising nutritional adequacy"

## When to Be More Cautious

- **Diagnosed FH** (familial hypercholesterolemia) — follow specialist advice
- **LDL-C requiring pharmacotherapy** — individual clinician guidance
- **Hyper-responders** (rare, ~25% of population) — dietary cholesterol has
  outsized effect on blood LDL

## Clinical Bottom Line

For most people, including those with mild-moderate LDL-C elevation,
**1–2 whole eggs/day in the context of a diet low in saturated fat and high
in fiber is not a concern.** The saturated fat in eggs is trivial compared to
the overall dietary pattern. Focus intervention on: reducing saturated fat
from red/processed meat and full-fat dairy; increasing fiber; and addressing
overweight/obesity.

But when a user asks about LARGE egg numbers, the binding constraint is
saturated fat, not cholesterol count. Per-egg values (both data sets agree on
SFA): 50 g egg ≈ 1.6 g SFA; cholesterol 186 mg (USDA FDC 171287, 372 mg/100 g)
vs 292 mg (中国食物成分表, 鸡蛋·白皮 585 mg/100 g). So 20 eggs ≈ 32 g SFA, i.e.
2.2× a 15 g/d budget, plus 3,700–5,850 mg cholesterol, ~1,400 kcal and ~127 g
protein (above a 70–90 g protein target, but it crowds out vegetables/fiber).
15 g SFA ≈ 9–10 eggs with no other SFA source that day.

A widely circulated "720 eggs in a month, LDL −20%" item (Norwitz, 2024) is a
n=1 self-experiment, not peer reviewed, with ~75 g/d SFA; the LDL fall tracked
the +60 g/d net carbs he added in the same weeks. Never cite it as evidence.

## Guidance for Panacea

- Do not recommend egg restriction based solely on cholesterol content
- Evaluate the full dietary context (what accompanies the eggs)
- For users with elevated LDL-C, address the major levers first: saturated fat
  sources, fiber, weight, physical activity
- If the user is on a statin or has very high LDL-C (≥4.9), advise clinician
  consultation for individualized guidance
