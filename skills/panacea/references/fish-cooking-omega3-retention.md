# Fish Cooking and Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) Retention

> Current as of 2026-08. Evidence-based guidance for Panacea dietary
> consultations on maximizing omega-3 from cooked fish.

## Core Mechanism

EPA and DHA are NOT destroyed by normal cooking temperatures. They are
**lost through fat rendering** — as the fish cooks, fat melts and carries
EPA/DHA out of the fillet into the pan, drip tray, or frying oil.

Key protective factors in whole fish:
- Omega-3s are sequestered in cellular membranes, not free-floating
- Fish muscle contains natural antioxidants (vitamin E, carotenoids)
- Internal temperature stays well below oven/pan surface temperature as long
  as moisture is retained

Post-mortem handling (room-temperature storage) degrades omega-3 more than
gentle cooking (2021 study on Atlantic mackerel).

## True Retention by Cooking Method

Based on Choo et al. 2018 (*ScienceAsia*), salmon — true retention adjusted
for cooking yield:

| Method | EPA+DHA Retained | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Steaming (蒸)** | **~65–70%** | Best; low temp, no fat loss |
| **Foil-baking (锡纸烤)** | **~60–65%** | Traps rendered fat + juices |
| Grilling (烤架) | ~35–40% | Fat drips into flames |
| Deep-frying (炸) | ~25–30% | Oil exchange + oxidation |

Other studies (concentration basis — flattering; read as "no dramatic loss"):
- Boiling/poaching: no significant drop per 100g (Leung 2018)
- Microwaving: no significant change per 100g (Bastías 2017)
- Canning: well retained (Bastías 2017, *J Food Sci Technol*)

## The "Mackerel Math"

Mackerel (鲭鱼/青花鱼) is so omega-3-dense that even the worst cooking
method still delivers:

| Mackerel State (100g) | EPA+DHA | % of 250mg/day minimum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Raw / properly cooked | ~2,500–4,500 mg | ~1,000% |
| Deep-fried (~75% loss) | ~625–1,125 mg | ~250% |

**Bottom line:** a 100g portion of mackerel clears the daily requirement
however you cook it.

## Practical Recommendations

### Best Methods (ranked)
1. **Steaming** — zero fat loss, simplest
2. **Foil-baking (en papillote)** — traps all juices; 200°C, 10–15 minutes
3. **Poaching** — low-temp water/broth
4. **Microwaving** — fastest, cover to retain moisture

### If Pan-Frying is Unavoidable
- Use minimal oil
- Keep time short, medium heat
- **Do not discard the pan juices** — they contain rendered omega-3
- Pour juices back over the fish or into accompanying grains/vegetables

### Practices That Cost the Most Omega-3
- Deep-frying
- High-heat pan-frying until crispy
- Grilling without foil (fat drips away)
- **Pouring off rendered fat/juices** (this is exactly where the omega-3 went)

## Canned Fish

Canned fatty fish (mackerel, sardines, salmon) retains omega-3 well:
- Canning uses steaming/pressure cooking — a gentle method
- Choose water-packed or olive-oil-packed over vegetable-oil-packed
- The canning liquid contains water-soluble nutrients; consider consuming it
- Unsalted water-packed varieties have the cleanest ingredient profile

## Guidance for Panacea

- When users report "fish oil loss during cooking," explain the fat-rendering
  mechanism (not heat destruction)
- Recommend steaming or foil-baking as default methods
- Remind users that mackerel/salmon are so omega-3-rich that even with losses
  they still hit targets
- For users with lipid concerns, note that gentler cooking methods also
  preserve the fish's natural antioxidant profile
