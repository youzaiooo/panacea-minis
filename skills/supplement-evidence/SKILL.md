# Supplement Evidence Evaluation

Use this Skill for evidence-based evaluation of dietary supplements — fish oil, plant oils, vitamins, and other common supplements. Provides selection frameworks, product label analysis, and dosage guidance backed by clinical guidelines and third-party testing standards.

## When to Use

- User asks "what fish oil should I buy" or how to evaluate a supplement
- User shares a product image/label and asks "is this good?"
- User asks about supplement dosage, form differences, or quality standards
- User asks about plant oils (perilla, flaxseed, etc.) as omega-3 sources

## Fish Oil Evaluation Framework

See `references/fish-oil-selection-guide.md` for the complete evidence-based framework covering:
- EPA+DHA content vs capsule weight (purity calculation)
- Molecular form comparison (rTG > TG > EE) with bioavailability evidence
- IFOS and other third-party certification standards
- Oxidation/rancidity risks and freshness checks
- Dosage by clinical indication (AHA, GOED)
- Chinese regulatory categories (保健食品 vs 普通食品/凝胶糖果)
- REDUCE-IT context and why it doesn't generalize to OTC low-dose supplements

### Quick Product Label Cross-Check

When evaluating a specific product from images:

1. **Compare outer box weight vs nutrition panel weight** — discrepancies often mean one lists net oil weight and the other lists total capsule weight. Cross-validate: (fat g per 100g) × total weight should equal net oil weight.
2. **Verify certifications** on the issuer's website (IFOS, ConsumerLab, USP) — do not trust a logo alone.
3. **Check product category** — 凝胶糖果 (GB17399) = regular food, not health food. Not disqualifying if backed by IFOS, but means lower regulatory oversight.
4. **Read the ingredients list** — minimal ingredients (oil + capsule shell only, no artificial additives) is a positive signal.
5. **Calculate true EPA+DHA per daily serving** — ignore "fish oil XXXmg" marketing; use the actual EPA% and DHA% from the label.

## Evidence Hierarchy for Supplements

1. Clinical guidelines (AHA, ESC, GOED) and professional society statements
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses (Cochrane)
3. Randomized controlled human trials — check population, dose, duration, comparator
4. Third-party testing reports (IFOS, Consumer Reports, ConsumerLab)
5. Animal/mechanistic studies (supporting, not primary)

Reject: manufacturer marketing claims without third-party verification, self-media reviews, general-audience articles.

## Key Pitfalls

- **Do not recommend specific brands** — provide criteria and let the user decide. The agent is an educator, not a commercial endorser.
- **Do not assume "imported = better"** — Consumer Reports (2026) found quality failures across both domestic and imported brands.
- **Do not equate ALA-rich plant oils with fish oil** — conversion efficiency to EPA/DHA is only 5–10%, making them complementary but not equivalent.
- **Always distinguish OTC supplements from prescription omega-3s** — the REDUCE-IT evidence (icosapent ethyl 4 g/d) applies to a specific clinical population and dose, not to general OTC use.
- **Cross-check label claims** — outer packaging and nutrition facts panel may use different weight bases (net oil vs total capsule). This is common and not fraud, but must be explained to the user.
