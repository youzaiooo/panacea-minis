# Energy Budget & Weight-Loss Evidence Bank

Cited numbers for calorie-target / deficit / "eat at BMR" discussions and the
brain/sleep energy-expenditure Q&As. Built 2026-08-25 when the user proposed
"只要吃够基础代谢就行" (eat at BMR, let activity create the deficit).

## Deficit targets and intake floors (verified citations)

- **NHLBI 1998 Clinical Guidelines** (Obesity Evidence Report, NIH Pub
  98-4083; PDF text verified): weight-loss programs should create a deficit of
  **500–1,000 kcal/day**; low-calorie diet (LCD) = **1,000–1,200 kcal/day
  women, 1,200–1,500 kcal/day men**.
- **2013 AHA/ACC/TOS obesity guideline** (Jensen et al., *Circulation*
  2014;129:S102–S138, doi:10.1161/01.cir.0000437739.71477.ee): prescribed
  energy restriction **1,200–1,500 women / 1,500–1,800 men** kcal/day.
  PMC/ahajournals are antibot-blocked; numbers cross-verified via AAFP 2016
  review and empr.com 2013 summary (cite as secondary corroboration).
- **NHLBI heart-healthy living page** (updated 2025-12-11): losing **3–5% of
  body weight lowers triglycerides and glucose**; 5–10% over ~6 months
  improves BP/LDL/HDL.
- Long-term caveat: the linear 3,500 kcal/lb rule overestimates loss because
  metabolism adapts (Hall KD et al., *Lancet* 2011;378:826–37).
- **eutils workaround**: PubMed/PMC web pages are cookie/reCAPTCHA-walled, but
  `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` esearch+efetch with a browser UA returns abstracts
  reliably — use it instead of giving up on a citation.

## BMR and the "eat-at-BMR floor" strategy

- Mifflin-St Jeor (men): 10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age + 5. Formula error ±10–15% —
  present as a floor range, never an exact value.
- Worked example (reference case): 171 cm / 67.5 kg / 27M → BMR ≈ 1,620 kcal;
  TDEE (sedentary 1.2–1.3×) ≈ 2,000 kcal; eating AT BMR → ~380–480 kcal/day
  deficit ≈ 0.3–0.35 kg/week — inside the safe 500–1,000 kcal band and within a
  0.3–0.5 kg/week goal. A 1,600–1,800 kcal daily target ≈ BMR.
- Communication pattern: endorse "BMR as floor,
  never below ~1,500 kcal (male guideline floor)"; protein and fiber
  (≥15g) targets are weight/lipid-based and do NOT scale down with calories;
  remind that sedentary TDEE already accounts for an "activity all day"
  intuition — don't double-count.

## Brain energy expenditure (thinking / gaming)

- Brain ≈ 2% body mass but ≈ **20% of RMR** (Raichle & Gusnard, *PNAS* 2002,
  PMC124895). Baseline ≈ 0.18 kcal/min at 1,300 RMR (~12 W).
- **Thinking adds negligible whole-body energy**: local glucose uptake rises
  but is tiny relative to the high maintenance baseline (Messier review +
  Kurzban, reported in Scientific American 2012-07-18 "Does Thinking Really
  Hard Burn More Calories?"). Frame: 高待机、低边际成本 — "a server that
  already idles expensively; one more process is nearly free".
- Practical trap: mental effort precedes **stress eating** — 45 min of
  cognitive tasks → +~200 kcal buffet intake with cortisol ↑ (small study of
  14 female students; PubMed 18725427, reported in SciAm 2012). Do NOT cite
  this as "JAMA 2018" — that was a misattribution; use the SciAm/PubMed
  18725427 trail. Advise guarding post-gaming/session snacking.

## Sleep energy expenditure

- Whole-body: sleep metabolic rate ≈ **15% below resting wakefulness**,
  circadian minimum in early morning (Sharma & Kavuru, *Int J Endocrinol*
  2010;2010:270832, PMC2929498); SMR vs BMR gap only ~5% (PMID 15895522).
- Brain is stage-dependent, not uniformly lower:
  - NREM/SWS = night's lowest: global CBF falls; largest regional decreases in
    pons/mesencephalon, thalami, basal ganglia, basal forebrain, orbitofrontal,
    anterior cingulate, precuneus (Maquet et al., *J Neurosci* 1997;17:2807–12,
    PMID 9092602).
  - REM = reactivation to waking-like levels in limbic regions (Maquet et al.,
    *Prog Brain Res* 2005;150:219–27, PMID 16186026).
  - During sleep the brain partially switches from glucose to fatty acids /
    ketones (*Cerebral metabolic changes during sleep*, PMC6688614).
- Practical: sleep deprivation does NOT create a worthwhile calorie surplus —
  it raises next-day intake (ghrelin/leptin) and lowers activity; keep 7–8h as
  part of weight management. All brain/sleep costs are already inside BMR.
